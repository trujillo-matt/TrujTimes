News

Stock market

CNBC / Yahoo Finance — Stocks whipsawed through the week: Tuesday's session fell as renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities pushed oil prices higher, before Wednesday's cooling Treasury yields lifted the S&P 500 and Nasdaq roughly 0.5% each. Thursday brought the sharpest move, with the Dow jumping 624 points (1.2%) to 53,686.11 and the S&P and Nasdaq both gaining more than 1%, as Fed Governor Christopher Waller signaled he'd favor holding rates steady barring an inflation surprise. The 10-year Treasury yield still touched 4.818% on Wednesday, its highest since November 2023, after August nonfarm payrolls grew by 162,000 — triple the 53,000 economists expected — and pushed fed-funds futures to price a 58% chance of a rate hike. Friday gave some of the week's gains back, with the Dow closing at 53,414.25, the S&P 500 at 7,718.60, and the Nasdaq at 26,506.99.

Geopolitics

Just Security / Security Council Report — Tension with Iran stayed front and center: President Trump said the U.S. would hit back "hard" after Iranian attacks on bases in Jordan, while downplaying talk of a return to full-scale war and calling Iran a "failed nation." At the UN, France took over the Security Council presidency for September and is expected to bring a vote on renewing Sudan's sanctions regime before it lapses on September 12. Diplomats are also weighing candidates for the next UN Secretary-General amid what analysts describe as rising strain on multilateral institutions. September's calendar still points toward a Xi-Trump summit alongside UN General Assembly, SCO, and BRICS+ meetings, all set against the backdrop of the unresolved U.S.-Iran confrontation.

Technology

Tech Startups / CNBC — Nvidia agreed to its largest deal ever this week, buying AI platform Hugging Face for roughly $12.9 billion in cash plus about $1 billion in retention equity; Hugging Face says its hub will stay open to competing models and chips, though the deal will test how independent it can remain inside Nvidia. Broadcom separately reported AI-chip revenue up 221% year over year, and China's Moonshot AI is reportedly preparing a Hong Kong IPO near a $50 billion valuation. Tesla began running wheel-free Cybercabs on Austin streets and almost immediately drew a federal safety probe. At the G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, member countries endorsed non-binding, U.S.-backed AI policy principles favoring light-touch, sector-specific regulation.

AI

Tech Startups / CNBC — OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra this week, rolling it out over several days to paid ChatGPT plans, its API, Azure, and Bedrock, with the company framing the release as the arrival of an "AGI era." Anthropic said Claude worked largely autonomously over 11 days on the Prove2Me platform to produce the first end-to-end, computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean programming language. At the same Chapel Hill summit, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick publicly praised Anthropic, telling the audience "we trust" the lab and that it's "back on the right side" with the administration. Separately, Nvidia researchers reported their Nemotron-3-Ultra-CC model scored 535.4 out of 600 on the IOI 2026 programming olympiad problem set, ahead of the top human contestant's 498.27.

Pokémon investing

TCGIndex — The Pokémon TCG remains the largest single trading-card game within the roughly $8–9 billion global card market, holding an estimated 12% share, and as of September 4 the tracker had 12 of the 14 games it follows trending up on the week. Chase cards — Special Illustration Rares, Immersive Rares, and select vintage sealed product — continue to command premiums even as modern common cards stay oversupplied after The Pokémon Company printed 11.9 billion cards in fiscal 2023–24. Market watchers describe the space as stabilized since its pandemic-era boom and correction, with current guidance favoring graded chase cards and select vintage sealed product over mass-market singles.

Sports

F.C. Barcelona

ESPN — Barcelona survived an early scare to beat Rayo Vallecano 5-2 last Monday, falling behind in the 12th minute to a Sergio Camello finish before Raphinha equalized in the 19th and Lamine Yamal put the hosts ahead in the 21st. Rayo pulled within one when Camello headed home a corner in the 59th, but Barcelona pulled away with a Florian Lejeune own goal, a Raphinha finish off an Anthony Gordon fast break in the 71st, and a stoppage-adjacent Lamine Yamal strike from outside the box in the 90th, assisted by Karim Adeyemi. Lamine Yamal and Raphinha each scored twice in the win.

Miami Hurricanes

ESPN / State of The U / Yahoo Sports — Miami opened its season with a 45-6 rout at Stanford behind a spectacular debut from transfer quarterback Darian Mensah, who went 27-of-30 for 401 yards and five touchdowns. Malachi Toney set Miami's single-game receiving record with 12 catches for 234 yards and three touchdowns, including a 47-yard score in the third quarter, while Cooper Barkate added a 34-yard touchdown catch. Miami led 17-0 after the first quarter on two Mensah-to-Toney touchdowns and an Emmet Kenney field goal, and never let Stanford threaten; Jake Weinberg closed the scoring with a 46-yard field goal in the fourth. Stanford managed only two field goals from Emmet Kenney and was outgained badly, with quarterback Davis Warren going 21-of-41 for 214 yards.
