News

Stock market

CNBC / Bloomberg — Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter report Wednesday blew past expectations — revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year, and guidance for roughly 70% growth in fiscal 2028 versus the 44% Wall Street had penciled in — sending the stock up nearly 9% and lifting chipmakers Broadcom and Intel alongside it. The rally faded some by Friday after new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, in his first Jackson Hole keynote, called inflation still "uncomfortably high" and signaled the Fed has more work to do, pushing the market-implied odds of a September rate hike from roughly 35% to above 55%. Treasury yields rose in response, with the 10-year near 4.72%, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each slipped Friday. Even so, all three major indexes closed the week higher — the Dow's first winning week in three — with the S&P up about 0.5%, the Nasdaq up 0.9%, and the Dow near 53,560.

Geopolitics

Al Jazeera / NPR / Kyiv Independent — The Gaza ceasefire looked shakier by week's end: Hamas accused Israel of using "fake pretexts" to keep fighting, Israel threatened a forceful response after Hamas revived incendiary-kite attacks, and the U.S.-backed Board of Peace's Gaza envoy warned a full collapse would be a "point of no return," all following Prime Minister Netanyahu's public rejection of President Trump's 15-point peace plan. In Ukraine, Russia struck warehouse facilities in Kyiv Oblast on Friday, briefly closing a major highway to western Ukraine, while Italy pledged four SAMP/T NG air-defense batteries, Aster 30 interceptor missiles, and seven long-range radars. CIA Director John Ratcliffe made a previously undisclosed trip to Moscow, reportedly prompted by a classified assessment that Vladimir Putin senses U.S. weakness in the stalled peace talks; the Kremlin said Ratcliffe did not meet Putin and called it premature to read anything into the visit. U.S.-mediated negotiations remain stuck over territorial concessions and security guarantees.

Technology

NBC News / ABC News / MacRumors / CNBC — Meta agreed this week to pay up to $16.7 billion settling child-safety claims from a 29-state coalition, and must now add daily time caps and an overnight usage block for teens on Facebook and Instagram, along with stronger age verification and parental controls; the company denies wrongdoing but had faced potential exposure topping $1 trillion had it lost at trial. Apple confirmed hardware chief John Ternus will succeed Tim Cook as CEO on September 1, ending Cook's 15-year run atop the company, the same week Apple announced new Mac mini and Mac Studio models and confirmed a September 9 event expected to unveil a foldable iPhone, iPhone 18 Pro models, and new Apple Watches. And Amazon said it's shutting down Mechanical Turk, its 21-year-old crowdsourced task marketplace, on September 30, citing the rise of AI-era data-labeling firms like Scale AI and Mercor that have taken over its niche.

AI

Bloomberg / CNBC / TIME — Anthropic committed roughly $45 billion over six years to UK firm Nscale for about 460 megawatts of data-center capacity at a planned West Virginia campus running on Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips, days after Microsoft reportedly exited as the project's anchor tenant. TIME reported OpenAI is resetting strategy under Sam Altman — winding down its Sora video app, a Disney partnership, and the standalone Atlas browser to refocus resources — amid reporting that Anthropic has overtaken it in annualized revenue and private valuation; OpenAI is also retiring the DALL-E model from ChatGPT on Sunday and cut its GPT-5.6 API pricing by more than 20%. It was a financial and infrastructure week for AI rather than a research one — no major new frontier model shipped, but Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate was reported to have passed $65 billion, with investors reportedly eyeing a roughly $2 trillion IPO valuation this fall.

Sports

F.C. Barcelona

ESPN / Barca Blaugranes — Barcelona opened its LaLiga campaign with two comfortable wins this week, thrashing Elche 5-0 last Sunday and beating Athletic Club 2-0 in Wednesday's home opener at a reopened Camp Nou. Raphinha was the standout at Elche, converting a 14th-minute penalty and later setting up Karim Adeyemi before adding a goal of his own, with Fermin Lopez capping the rout with a brace late on; Raphinha then opened the scoring against Athletic Club in the 37th minute off a Pedri through ball, with Fermin Lopez sealing it in the 82nd. The only scare of the week came when Gavi went down clutching his surgically repaired knee during the Elche match — medical tests found only a bruise, and he's already back in full training. Barcelona sits 2-0-0 to start the season.

Minnesota Vikings

ESPN / Star Tribune / Heavy.com — The Vikings closed out the preseason with two lopsided losses, falling 13-3 to Baltimore last Saturday and 34-6 at Denver on Friday, with backups doing the passing since Kyler Murray was already locked in as the Week 1 starter. Denver's Sam Ehlinger torched Minnesota's defense for 233 yards and three touchdown passes, including an 88-yard strike to Justin Joly, while Minnesota managed only two Will Reichard field goals in the finale. The results matter less than Sunday's 5 p.m. deadline to cut from roughly 90 players to a 53-man roster: wide receiver Jeshaun Jones will serve a three-game suspension starting on cutdown day, quarterback J.J. McCarthy was banged up with an ankle injury against Baltimore, and rookie tackle Caleb Tiernan and safety Theo Jackson are both working toward being ready for the September 13 opener against Green Bay.
